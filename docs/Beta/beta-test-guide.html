Ritzy Studio turns a photograph of a real room into a client-ready interior design — a concept rendered from three consistent camera angles, matched to real, buyable furniture from Dubai retailers, within the client's budget, and presented the way a designer would present it. This guide walks you through the complete journey as its first admin tester, and shows you where the money — both spend and revenue — is visible.

N° 01 — The journey you are testing
iPhotographThe client uploads 2–3 photos of the room from different corners. The first photo's perspective anchors every render.
iiBriefStyles, inspiration images, budget, colours to use and to avoid, measurements, and room planning — focal point, seating priority, areas to keep clear.
iiiClarifying questionsThe studio asks a short set of tailored questions before it designs, like a designer would.
ivConceptA photorealistic concept of the actual room — architecture preserved, brief respected — plus two additional camera angles of the same designed room.
vShopping listReal products from the 3,300-piece catalogue, matched to the concept's palette and roles, quantity-aware within budget, with swappable alternatives.
viFinal renderThe room re-rendered with the actual purchased pieces, quality-checked for spatial errors, again from three angles: hero, reverse, and a close detail vignette.
viiRoom preview & unlockThe client-facing reveal. Unlocking (AED 99 via Stripe) opens the full shopping list with retailer links.

N° 02 — What makes this beta-ready

Renders survive anything. The final render runs on durable background infrastructure. The client can close the tab, lose connection, or put their phone away the moment they tap "Generate" — the render completes on its own and retries itself on failure. We proved this in production by killing the browser five seconds after triggering: the finished room was waiting when we came back.

The list is the truth. Every item on the shopping list is a real product at a real retailer. The render is a faithful composition of those items — never the other way around. Budgets are enforced on real line totals, including quantities, and "avoid" colours are excluded outright.

Every dirham of AI spend is recorded. Each image generation logs its exact gateway cost to the database as it happens — page 4 shows you where to read it.

N° 04 — The test script
1
Sign up, confirm, and complete onboarding

Create your account, confirm via email, sign in, and answer the onboarding questions.

Check
  • A non-ritzyinteriors email is refused with the "internal pilot" message.
  • After sign-in you land on onboarding, then an empty dashboard that feels intentional, not broken.
2
Create a project and a room

Create a project with a client name, then add a room. Start with Living Room; on your second pass, try Living & Dining — the combined hall is a signature case for Dubai apartments.

3
Upload the room photos

Upload 2–3 corner shots. The page guides corner-by-corner capture.

Check
  • Uploads complete with visible previews; removing and re-adding a photo works.
4
Complete the brief — and try to trip it

Work through all four brief steps: styles, inspiration, details, and the clarifying questions. In details, set a realistic budget (e.g. AED 40,000), name colours you love, and — importantly — name a colour to avoid (e.g. "avoid red"). Add measurements if you have them (recommended, not required), and answer the room-planning questions: focal point, seating priority, what must stay clear.

Check
  • The clarifying questions arrive within ~30 seconds and actually reflect your brief.
  • Skipping measurements does not block you — the studio notes its assumption instead.
5
Generate the concept

Expect 1–2 minutes for the hero image, with the two extra camera angles appearing shortly after.

Check
  • Your room's actual architecture — windows, doors, ceiling, built-ins — is preserved.
  • The design honours the brief, and your avoided colour is nowhere in it.
  • All three angles show the same designed room: same furniture, palette, and layout.
  • The "what we assumed" notes read honestly.

6
Match products and review the shopping list

Select the concept and let the studio source real products (1–3 minutes). Review the selected pieces and browse the alternatives for each role; swap one or two.

Check
  • Every item is a real product with a real retailer, class-correct for its role.
  • The total — including quantities (two armchairs count twice) — sits within your budget.
  • Nothing in the selected list or the alternatives wears your avoided colour.
  • The pieces feel palette-coherent with the concept image, not randomly assorted.
7
Generate the final render — then walk away

Click Generate final render, and once you land on the preview page, close the tab. Make coffee. Come back after five minutes and reopen the room. This is deliberate: the render must complete without you.

Check
  • The hero render is there, followed by the reverse angle and a close detail vignette.
  • The rendered furniture matches the shopping list — same sofa, same rug, same lamp.
  • The detail angle is genuinely a close-up (a sofa end, tabletop styling, rug texture), not a second wide shot.
8
Exercise the recovery path

Renders retry themselves on failure, so a stuck spinner should be rare. If a render ever runs past ~4 minutes without a result, the page offers a retry — that affordance is designed recovery, not an error. Also try triggering a render twice in quick succession and confirm the studio tells you one is already running rather than starting a duplicate.

9
Unlock and follow the money

From the room preview, unlock the shopping list (AED 99 — Stripe is in test mode; use the test card from page 2). After payment you should land directly on the full shopping list.

Check
  • Payment completes and returns you to the app without dead ends.
  • Every product's retailer link opens the correct product page.
  • The payment appears in the Stripe dashboard.
10
Run a second room — the repetition test

Add a second room to the same project (try the combined Living & Dining, or a bedroom) and run the journey again, faster this time.

Check
  • The second room's furniture is not a repeat of the first room's pieces.
  • The combined hall reads as one coherent space with two zones, from all angles.
  • The dashboard now presents both rooms respectably.
N° 05 — When something goes wrong

Note the time, the room, the page URL, and take a screenshot, then continue if you can — failures are preserved server-side either way. Send findings to Ayo in one batch; copy and polish notes are as valuable as hard failures. Nothing you do can affect another user's data, so test without fear.

N° 06 — Where the money is visible

Ritzy Studio records the cost of every AI image it generates, at the moment it generates it. There are three ledgers, each behind its own dashboard login — access is granted by Ayo inviting your email to each.

LedgerWhere & what
AI spend
(per generation)		The application database — supabase.com → Ritzy Studio project. Every concept, camera angle, and revision writes its exact cost in USD to the ai_jobs table (cost_estimate_usd); each final render records its own cost on the render's record. Use the SQL editor with the queries below for daily or per-room totals.
AI credits
(the account)		evolink.ai — the gateway that serves our image and text models. The dashboard shows the live credit balance and per-model usage. 68 credits ≈ 1 USD; a full client journey costs ≈ 30 credits (≈ $0.45). Balance at time of writing: ≈ 549 credits. When it runs low, this is the account to top up.
Revenue & infradashboard.stripe.com for unlock payments and refunds. vercel.com for hosting and compute (the render queue runs here; its cost is usage-based and currently negligible).
Ready-made queries — Supabase SQL editor
-- AI spend by day (concepts, camera angles, revisions)
select date_trunc('day', created_at)::date as day,
       round(sum(cost_estimate_usd)::numeric, 2) as usd
from ai_jobs
where cost_estimate_usd is not null
group by 1 order by 1 desc;

-- Final-render spend by day
select date_trunc('day', created_at)::date as day,
       round(sum((input_summary->>'costEstimateUsd')::numeric), 2) as usd
from render_jobs
where input_summary ? 'costEstimateUsd'
group by 1 order by 1 desc;

-- The most expensive rooms this month
select room_id, round(sum((input_summary->>'costEstimateUsd')::numeric), 2) as usd
from render_jobs
where input_summary ? 'costEstimateUsd'
  and created_at > date_trunc('month', now())
group by 1 order by 2 desc limit 10;
N° 07 — The bar

The question behind every step of this script is the one we hold ourselves to: would a designer be proud to present this, and would a client actually buy it? Where the answer is no, that is the finding we want most.
