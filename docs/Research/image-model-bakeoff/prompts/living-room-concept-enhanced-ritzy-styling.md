# Living Room Concept Prompt: Enhanced Ritzy Styling

Use the uploaded source room photo as the base image.

Create one photorealistic editorial residential interior photograph for a high-end but livable Dubai villa or large townhouse living room. This must feel like a finished interior designer scheme, not bare staging, not a CGI showroom, not an IKEA catalog, and not generic beige luxury.

Preserve the source room architecture and camera logic. Keep the visible windows, doors, ceiling planes, openings, columns, staircase if present, fireplace if present, built-ins, AC vents, sockets, fixed joinery, wall boundaries, floor boundary, and any other fixed architectural constraints. Do not invent fantasy architecture or move major openings. Improve the room through interior design, not by replacing the building.

Design a layered, complete living room:
- Create a credible residential seating group with a sofa and accent seating, arranged for conversation and scaled to the room.
- Specify materials and color families: tactile linen, boucle, velvet, wool, warm oak or walnut, travertine or limestone, aged brass or bronze, muted olive, warm ivory, tobacco, charcoal, or stone where appropriate.
- Use a rug that is large enough to anchor the full seating group, with front legs on the rug and believable clearance.
- Include a coffee table and side tables with finished styling: books, trays, ceramic vessels, branches or greenery, sculptural objects, cushions, and throws.
- Add layered lighting where architecturally appropriate: floor lamp, table lamps, sconces, picture lights, pendant, ceiling lighting, cove lighting, or art lighting. Make the lighting warm, practical, and physically plausible.
- Treat the walls intentionally. Add scaled artwork, mirror, paneling, shelves, picture light, or considered negative space. Avoid empty walls unless the restraint is clearly intentional and resolved.
- Add curtains or window treatment if the room needs softness, privacy, or a finished designer layer.
- Include a TV/media unit only if it makes sense from the source room and user intent. Do not force a TV into every room.

Make the image feel designed, layered, collected, and complete while staying livable. Use editorial residential photography with natural daylight, warm secondary lighting, realistic material texture, believable shadows, correct scale, and a camera perspective consistent with the source photo.

Avoid: hotel lobby sterility, overdecorated render fluff, generic beige luxury, rural/wilderness context, showroom matching, fantasy architecture, impossible reflections, warped furniture, distorted rugs, fake labels, price tags, logos, text, watermarks, or visible retailer claims.
