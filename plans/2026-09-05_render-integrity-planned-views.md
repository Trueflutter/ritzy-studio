# Render integrity and planned views (S4)

Slice S4 of `plans/2026-09-01_product-pass-implementation.md` (steps 13 and 14,
approved at Gate 1). Date: 2026-09-05. Branch: `fable/s4-render-integrity`.

## Objective

On the reveal, a homeowner sees her finished room from three planned viewpoints
that together show every key piece of the design and always the room's focal
element (the TV wall in a TV lounge), with the alternate wide angle standing
where one of her own photographs was taken when she gave more than one, and the
exact products she selected reproduced across the set. A render whose placement
review failed twice is shown with the review's findings and a working "render
again", never presented as finished; an angle that could not be verified as the
same room is left out and said so; and the SKU disclaimer sits beneath every
final render. The critique harness judges the final view set, not only the
concept hero, and records the app's own verdicts beside its own so the two
judges can be compared per room and per anchor.

## Approach

Final render views stop being two fixed keys. A pure planner in
`packages/domain` derives the view set from facts: the room type, the brief's
focal point, the confirmed spec's key roles, how many photographs the room has,
and one cheap vision read ("camera read") of the hero render and the
photographs: whether the hero shows the focal element, which key roles the hero
does not show, and for each photograph where its camera stands relative to the
hero camera and whether it faces the focal wall. The planner is deterministic
and unit-tested; the model only reads facts, it never decides the plan. The
vocabulary of view keys is closed (`focal_wide`, `reverse_wide`,
`anchor_detail`), so labels, storage paths and the presentation need no schema
change: the read and the plan are written once, in the hero's success write on
`render_jobs.input_summary` where the spatial QA verdicts already live, and each
view's outcome is an append-only `ai_jobs` row (the consistency check's own
audit row), so no two deliveries ever merge the same JSON document.

The camera read runs in the hero path, on each hero image before its spatial
QA, and its facts reach both judges. It also says, per photograph, whether the
photo is the same room as the hero at all; a photograph the read is not sure
about is never an anchor, however well it faces the focal wall. The QA is told whether the focal element
is in frame or behind the camera, and the escalation rule (below) applies only
when it is in frame, so a room whose TV wall backs the hero camera (Bolaji's
Al Furjan, Phase 0) is never regenerated for a wall the camera cannot see and
never carries a permanent flag for it; the focal view carries that wall
instead. The consistency check is told what each view is expected to show that
the hero may not, so the focal view is judged against the anchored photograph
and the expected roles rather than flagged as invention. When the read is unavailable (timeout, provider error), the planner is
conservative rather than inferential: a room with a known focal point gets the
focal view anyway, because a redundant angle of a wall the hero already shows
is the cheaper error, and a hero's focal visibility is never inferred from the
spec's role list. The read is what makes photo anchoring possible at all, and
with S5's three photo slots multi-photo rooms become the norm. Cost: one `gpt-5-mini` call at low image detail per
QA'd hero image, about USD 0.003, two per job in the regeneration case.

Each planned view is generated from up to three kinds of reference: the room
photograph whose camera the view stands at (architecture truth, when one
exists), the committed hero render (design truth), and the subset of selected
product photographs the view must show. Fewer obligations per image is the
roadmap's own argument (`brand/multi-view-render-roadmap`) and it is also the
mechanism the fill-rate finding needs later: a view built from a secondary
role's photograph is a view that role can be judged against. This slice builds
the mechanism and measures it; it does not anchor more roles.

The hero render itself gains what step 13 asks for: every room photograph as a
reference (today only the first), the spec's must-preserve list as a bounded
preservation contract in the prompt, product references limited to the
shopper's selected rows in render priority order, and a deadline, which it has
never had.

Spatial QA gains teeth in three places. The effective verdict is derived from
the per-check fields, so a hard violation (focal orientation with the focal
element in frame, blocked opening or impossible scale, or broken zoning in a
combined room) is a regeneration verdict even when the model itself says
"warn", which is exactly the Phase 0 failure. The one bounded regeneration
stays as it is. What changes is what happens after it: an unresolved verdict,
or a review that could not run, is recorded as an outcome the presentation
shows in the shopper's words, with the findings and a "render again" that
actually works for a succeeded job (today the action redirects an unpaid
succeeded selection to "Final render is ready"). A cross-view consistency check
judges each planned view against the hero and its anchored photograph (same
architecture, same shared objects, the expected roles present, nothing
invented); an inconsistent view gets one regeneration, and one that is still
inconsistent is stored for audit but excluded from the set the reveal shows,
with a one-line note. The queue contract is untouched: the hero path's claim,
success and failure writes do not change, and the views phase keeps today's
completion mechanism (an incomplete set below the attempt cap rethrows so the
queue redelivers and the next delivery repairs only what is missing). It stays
idempotent, and spend stays exclusive, the way the spec extraction already is:
a delivery opens a view's check row as a LEASE before any paid call, and one
additive partial unique index on `ai_jobs` lets exactly one live or succeeded
row exist per render job and view key, so a duplicate delivery hits a unique
violation and adopts instead of paying; a dead delivery's expired lease is
reclaimed by a status-conditional compare-and-swap, again as extraction does.
Every generated view is uploaded to an attempt-unique path with its own asset
row (never overwritten, so a reclaimed attempt can never replace bytes another
delivery has judged), the row records the asset it produced and the outcome it
gave that asset, and the view a reveal shows is the asset of the one succeeded
row for its key; the job's asset list is written under an optimistic version so
two deliveries recomputing it cannot leave a stale list standing. An unresolved view is terminal and can never cause
endless redelivery; a view that could not be started for lack of time is not
terminal, and redelivery completes it. Every paid call in the attempt, hero
included, runs against one absolute attempt deadline pinned under the queue
route's `maxDuration`: text calls take a per-request timeout bounded by what is
left, image calls take the remaining deadline, and each phase checks its
reserve before starting; the app's own stale threshold is pinned above that
budget, so a slow provider degrades to an honest outcome or a redelivery
instead of a platform kill or a self-inflicted reclaim.

The obvious alternative is a third fixed key ("focal view") and a prompt line
telling the model to show the TV wall. It fails the plan's own requirement:
without knowing whether the hero already shows the focal element and which
photograph faces it, a fixed key invents the wall exactly as the Phase 0
reverse view did. The other alternative, asking the vision model to plan the
views itself, is neither testable nor idempotent on redelivery, and it puts a
model verdict where a rule belongs. A third alternative, feeding the planner
only facts the pipeline already has (spec role presence for focal coverage,
the QA's own focal check), was considered: it cannot tell which photograph
faces the focal wall, and it leaves the QA blind to the camera, which is the
false regeneration the read prevents.

Two things from the first draft were cut on review. The concept stage keeps its
fixed two keys: the spec does not exist there, the deferred task has no budget
of its own inside the concepts route, and both callers would change for a
focal guarantee the final path now provides; the evidence run still generates
the concept views for the harness rooms (the current concepts have none), so
the existing concept `view_coverage` check is judged on a real set. And a
`zone_wide` key was dropped: the existing combined-hall reverse camera already
stands in the dining zone looking back, so the dining roles are assigned to
`reverse_wide` and the harness's coverage check reports whether that held.

Two open measurements from the handover are folded in without tuning anything:
the harness prints, per anchor, its own similarity beside the app's design
check score for the same concept and product (the sourcing job's recorded
verification verdicts, which score every judged product, not only the kept
ones); and the S4 evidence run judges identical images twice so judge variance
has a first number. Re-anchoring with more roles is measured on a throwaway
branch only if the evidence budget allows (last step, droppable), and nothing
from it is merged.

## Steps

1. **Planner** (`packages/domain/src/view-planning.ts`, pure). Closed vocabulary `focal_wide | reverse_wide | anchor_detail` with one label table used by both pages. `RoomCameraRead` (hero: shows focal element or null when unknown, hidden key role keys; per photo: same room as the hero as yes, unsure or no, camera relative to the hero camera as same, opposite, left, right or unknown, shows focal wall; `source: "vision" | "fallback"`). Only a photo whose same-room answer is yes is eligible as an anchor; the plan records why a photo was passed over. Photographs are identified by `room_assets.id` throughout (the read, the plan's `heroPhotoAssetId` and each view's `sourcePhotoAssetId`), never by position: the primary photograph is the first by `created_at` today, and when S5 gives the slots a stored order that contract replaces the ordering rule without touching persisted plans. `focalRoleKeysFor(focalPoint, roomType)` maps the brief's focal point to raw spec role vocabulary (`tv_media_wall` to tv, television and media console roles, `bed_wall` to bed and headboard, `workstation` to desk, `fireplace`, `art_display_wall`, `view_window`); the focal token is matched on raw spec roles, so an unsourceable role such as `tv` still counts. Key roles are the spec's sourceable roles from `sourcingRolesFromDesignSpec`, so built-ins and appliances never count as coverage; with a null spec the key roles are the selected products' roles. `planViews` rules, in order: the hero (photo 0's camera) covers every key role except the read's hidden ones; if the focal element is not covered and the focal point is known, the wide view is `focal_wide` anchored to the eligible photo that shows the focal wall (preferring an opposite camera), else unanchored; otherwise the wide view is `reverse_wide` anchored to an opposite photo when one exists; in a combined hall the dining key roles are assigned to the wide view's `mustShow` (the reverse camera stands in the dining zone). `anchor_detail` follows whenever the spec has at least one small-scale key role (side tables, lamps, decor, textiles) or the selected products exceed the hero's reference cap, which every confirmed spec in the harness satisfies; it is never photo-anchored. Every hidden key role is assigned to exactly one planned view's `mustShow` by scale class; the focal token is in the hero's coverage or the wide view's `mustShow`; the plan records what remains uncovered. Product references per view: the hero keeps the top eight by render priority; the wide view gets the products in its `mustShow` roles plus the primary seating or bed for continuity, capped at six; the detail view gets the products in its roles, capped at six; tokens with no product are ignored for references. `fallbackCameraRead` (the read timed out or failed) marks the hero's focal visibility unknown, marks every photo unknown and ineligible, and `planViews` treats unknown as not covered: with a known focal point the wide view is an unanchored `focal_wide`, and only with an unknown focal point does the set fall back to today's (`reverse_wide` then `anchor_detail`). Focal visibility is never inferred from the spec's role list. Test first: the fixtures in the acceptance criteria.

2. **Prompts and reads** (`packages/prompts`, `packages/ai`). Camera read prompt with strict JSON schema mirroring `RoomCameraRead` (including the per-photo same-room answer); runs on `gpt-5-mini` at low image detail with the hero and up to three photos, the focal point, and the key roles' labels; the ai package returns facts plus cost, and the caller opens its own `ai_jobs` row first (`render_camera_read`; no row, no call). The read, the QA and the consistency check each take a per-request `timeoutMs` (the pattern the sourcing, verification, anchor and palette calls already use), bounded by the caller to what is left of the attempt. View consistency prompt and schema: inputs are the hero, the view, the anchored photograph when the view has one, the view's expected roles (labels the hero may not show) and the hero's hidden roles; output `architectureConsistent` (against the anchored photo when given, else the hero's visible architecture), `cameraMatchesAnchor` (yes, no, or not applicable when the view has no anchored photo: does the view stand where the photograph was taken), `sharedObjectsConsistent`, `expectedShown`, `expectedMissing`, `invented` (purchasable pieces neither in the hero nor expected), `verdict`, `issues`; the verdict is `inconsistent` when architecture or shared objects disagree, when the anchored camera is not matched, when anything is invented, or when a focal expectation is missing. Spatial QA prompt version `2026-09-05.1`: the hard violations are named as `regenerate`, the context states whether the focal element is in frame or behind the camera, and the effective verdict is computed in code regardless. Camera language for `focal_wide` per room type in `interior-design-language.ts`; photo-anchored language stating that the first image is the real room photographed from where this view's camera stands and is the truth for walls, openings and proportions, and that the second image is the design to reproduce from that camera. Final render prompt: the multi-photo sentence already used by the concept prompt, and a preservation contract block rendered from the spec's must-preserve list bounded to eight entries of at most 120 characters each; the budget becomes 16,000 characters and `spatialQaCorrectionLanguage` is capped (at most four issues of 160 characters, about 960 characters in all), with a test pinning budget plus maximum suffix at or below the concept prompt's 17,000 envelope, which is the same Evolink token cap proven by `initial-concept-prompt-budget.test.ts`. The product summary is trimmed by dropping whole trailing lines (the list is priority-ordered), never through `truncateForPrompt`'s whitespace collapse. Budget test measures the real skeleton and asserts the worst case (three photos, full intent, eight products with dimensions, the 600-character concept notes, eight bounded preserve entries) fits with all eight product lines and all eight preserve entries intact.

3. **Enforcement module** (`apps/web/lib/render-qa.ts`, pure). `effectiveSpatialQaVerdict(qa, facts)`: `regenerate` when `scalePlausibility` or `zoning` is `fail`, or when `focalOrientation` is `fail` and the camera read says the focal element is in frame; otherwise the model's verdict (a focal fail with the element out of frame stays `warn` and is carried in the issues). `enforceSpatialQa({ render, assess, correction, remainingMs })`: assess (the caller's assess runs the camera read then the QA and returns both); on `regenerate`, render once with the correction suffix and assess again, only when the remaining budget covers an image call plus a read and a QA (the retry reserve), else record `unresolved` with reason `no_time_for_retry`; keep the retry only when its effective verdict is not `regenerate`; outcomes `passed | resolved_after_regeneration | unresolved | unreviewed`; returns the kept result and its facts, `regenerated` (true only when the retry was kept), issues, image credits including a discarded retry, text cost. `enforceViewConsistency({ generate, assess, correction, remainingMs })`: the same shape with outcomes `consistent | resolved_after_regeneration | unresolved | unchecked`; the unresolved case keeps the last attempt's image and both verdicts' issues. Test first against fakes (AC 5, 6).

4. **Hero path** (`apps/web/lib/render-runner.ts`, HIGH; `apps/web/lib/render-inputs.ts`; `apps/web/lib/render.ts`). `runFinalRender({ renderJobId, attempt }, deps?)` with lazy defaults: the service client factory and `revalidatePath` are resolved by dynamic import inside the function so a tsx test can import the module (the same device `enqueueFinalRender` already uses for the queue SDK); the consumer route's call is unchanged. The attempt budget is `FINAL_RENDER_ATTEMPT_BUDGET_MS` (700 seconds) in queue mode and `FINAL_RENDER_INLINE_BUDGET_MS` (285 seconds) in inline mode: the inline fallback runs inside whichever page route posted the action, so the three routes that post it (product-matching, shopping-list, presentation) all declare `maxDuration = 300`, and the test reads all three literals and pins the inline budget plus reserve under each. One absolute deadline is computed when the claim succeeds and threaded through every call: image calls receive the remaining time as `deadlineMs` (the helper `imageCallTimeoutMs` already caps it at the provider ceiling), text calls receive `timeoutMs = min(stage default, remaining)` per request, and each phase checks its reserve before starting (hero: any remaining; retry: image plus read plus QA; each view: image plus check; a view regeneration: image plus check). A phase that cannot fit records its honest state instead of running past the route: a hero retry that cannot fit is `unresolved` with reason `no_time_for_retry` (terminal; the shopper can render again); a planned view that cannot be started is left not started (not terminal), and in queue mode the views phase's existing incomplete-set rethrow asks the queue to redeliver, so the next delivery generates it under a fresh budget; only after the attempt cap does an incomplete set stand, and the reveal shows what exists. The arithmetic is stated rather than hidden: at every provider maximum (a 240 second image call, 90 second text calls) a single delivery cannot hold the hero, a hero retry, two views and their checks, and the hero retry takes priority because the hero commits once while views are repairable by redelivery; at the primary provider's observed 20 to 60 seconds per image the whole set completes in one delivery. A sequencing test with a fake clock, in which every call consumes its maximum, proves the attempt finishes inside the budget, commits the hero, leaves the unstarted views non-terminal, and requests redelivery on an attempt below the cap. `FINAL_RENDER_STALE_MS` rises to the queue budget plus a 60 second reserve (13 minutes), pinned by test; the presentation and the action read the same constant, so the app never reclaims an attempt that is still inside its budget. The consequence is stated: an inline-mode task that truly died shows its retry affordance after 13 minutes instead of 4; in queue mode a dead delivery is redelivered by the queue after 60 seconds, so the stale reclaim is only the inline backstop. `render-inputs.ts` loads photographs (up to three, `created_at` ascending), the parsed confirmed spec for the job's concept, and the selected products in render priority order, and returns the render input; the runner passes it with the deadline to `generateFinalGroundedRender`, replaces the inline QA loop with `enforceSpatialQa` (assess = camera read then QA with the facts), records `spatialQaOutcome`, `cameraRead` and the plan computed from the kept hero's facts in the success write beside the existing `spatialQaVerdict`, `spatialQaIssues` and `spatialQaRegenerated`, and hands the views phase the deadline. The claim CAS, the success CAS, the failure CAS, attempt-unique storage paths and the redelivery rules are not edited; the new runner test records the double's calls and pins their filters.

5. **Views phase** (`apps/web/lib/render-views.ts`, called from the runner's `runViewsPhase` with the identical `{ complete }` contract). Per delivery: read the job and its plan (`input_summary.viewPlan`), the job's view assets (`room_assets` with `asset_type: "final_render"` whose `storage_path` starts with the job's view prefix) and the view check rows (`ai_jobs` with `job_type: "render_view_check"` and `input_summary` containing `{ renderJobId, viewKey }`). One row per key is the rule the index enforces: at most one `running` or `succeeded` row per (render job, view key). A view is terminal when it has a `succeeded` row; the asset that row names is the one the reveal shows. For each planned view without a succeeded row: if a `running` row exists whose `leaseUntil` is in the future, another delivery owns it; skip it this delivery (not terminal). If a `running` row exists past its lease, reclaim it by a compare-and-swap (`status = running` to `failed`, `error_message` naming the expired lease), then proceed as if no row existed. Otherwise, when the remaining budget covers an image call and a check, open the row (`status: running`, `leaseUntil` = the attempt deadline plus 30 seconds, `input_summary: { renderJobId, viewKey, attempt }`); a unique violation on that insert means a concurrent delivery got there first: adopt, skip. Holding the lease: if the reclaimed row had recorded an `assetId` (the previous delivery died between upload and check), reuse that asset instead of generating; otherwise generate with references [anchored photo when planned, hero, product subset], upload to an attempt-unique path (`final-<job>-<key>-<nonce>.png`, the hero's own device) and insert its asset row with `view_key` set (never overwritten; the dashboard cover query excludes any asset with a view key, so an audit copy can never become a cover), and record `assetId` on the running row. Then run `enforceViewConsistency` with the expected roles and the anchored photo (a regeneration is a second attempt-unique asset and the row is updated to name the one kept), and close the row with a status-conditional write (`status = running`): `succeeded` carrying the outcome (`consistent | resolved_after_regeneration | unresolved | unchecked`), the issues, `regenerated`, the final `assetId` and the check's cost; a generation that throws closes it `failed`, which frees the key for the next delivery. A view left unstarted for lack of budget has no row: not terminal, not `complete`, and in queue mode the runner's existing rethrow below the attempt cap asks for redelivery. Recompute `output_asset_ids` as the hero plus each planned view's succeeded asset whose outcome is `consistent`, `resolved_after_regeneration` or `unchecked`, in plan order, and write it under an optimistic version: the update carries `input_summary.viewsVersion + 1` and filters on the version it read (`input_summary->>viewsVersion`) and on `status = succeeded`; zero rows means another delivery wrote first, so re-read and recompute, at most three times. `complete` is true when every planned view is terminal. Legacy jobs (succeeded under the old code, no persisted plan) take a compatibility plan (the old keys, hero reference only, no photo anchoring, no paid read) in two cases: when every old-key asset exists at its old deterministic path, adopt them as `unchecked` with no generation and no check, and they count as terminal without a row; when some are missing (the delivery died after the hero), generate the missing ones through the same lease, attempt-unique paths and check. Concurrency, stated exactly: the index makes paid generation exclusive per view key; the one residual duplicate is a reclaimed lease whose owner was alive but slower than its lease (bounded by one extra generation, and its asset is never overwritten, only unreferenced). The `final_render_views` job closes `succeeded` when every planned view is terminal (an `unresolved` verdict is a verdict, not a failure of the job) and `failed` when a view could not be produced or was left unstarted; its cost sums image credits and the checks' text cost.

## Rollout & rollback

- One additive migration, applied to the hosted project before the code that relies on it: a partial unique index on `ai_jobs` for `render_view_check` rows. It touches no column and no row, no rows of that type exist yet, and its creation cannot fail on existing data; the old code never inserts rows of that type, so the index is inert until the new code deploys. Reversal is `drop index`. No data backfill. The read, the plan and the versions are JSON in columns that already carry job metadata; the new `ai_jobs` job types are strings on a free-text column.
- Ordering: migration, then one PR, one deploy. In-flight jobs across the deploy are safe in both directions: a job whose hero committed under the old code and whose delivery dies is repaired by the new views phase through the compatibility plan (old keys; existing assets adopted as `unchecked` with no paid read or check; missing ones generated from the hero alone and checked); a job created under the new code and redelivered to the old code would regenerate the two old keys at deterministic paths, which the new code then adopts. Storage paths for the old keys are unchanged.
- Flags: none added. `RITZY_RENDER_EXECUTION` and the signup allowlist are untouched. The two new text stages follow the documented per-stage override contract (`RITZY_TEXT_MODEL_CAMERA_READ`, `RITZY_TEXT_EFFORT_CAMERA_READ`, and the same for `VIEW_CONSISTENCY`), defaulting to `gpt-5-mini` at low effort.
- Reversal: revert the PR and, if wanted, drop the index; leaving it in place is harmless. Jobs that recorded a read, a plan or outcomes keep working under the old presentation (it reads `output_asset_ids` and ignores unknown `input_summary` keys); views stored under `focal_wide` are labelled "Alternate angle" by the old label fallback; a `retryOf` field posted to the old action is ignored; the old 4 minute stale threshold returns with the revert.
- Cost and time guard: the camera reads and the consistency checks run on the cheapest stage model; each is one call per image judged; a failed read or check degrades to the fallback plan or the `unchecked` outcome, never to a retry loop; every paid call in the attempt runs against the attempt budget pinned under the route's `maxDuration`, a regeneration is skipped with an honest outcome when the budget cannot hold it, and the stale threshold is pinned above the budget so the app cannot reclaim a live attempt. The per-room evidence figure is recorded in Verification and compared to the USD 1.00 ceiling.
- Queue durability is asserted, not assumed: the consumer route and `vercel.json` are byte-identical, the runner's CAS filters are pinned by the new runner test, the views phase's `complete` and redelivery semantics are pinned by AC 7 and AC 14, and no view asset is ever overwritten after it exists.

## Deviations

- Step 2, measured on the real skeleton rather than the round numbers the plan carried: the production final render skeleton for a combined hall is 11,407 characters (12,031 with the dev-only strict preservation layer), eight full product lines are 2,279, and the largest retry suffix is 893. The budget is therefore 16,050 (not 16,000; 16,050 plus 893 stays under the 17,000 envelope), the preservation contract is bounded to six entries of 110 characters (not eight of 120; the walk room's confirmed list has five), and the concept notes the render carries are capped at 400 characters (not 600; the concept image is the design truth). With those bounds the worst case keeps all eight product lines with dimensions and every bounded preserve entry, which is what AC 9 asks for.
- Step 4, render priority order: the final render sorted its product references by render priority only behind `RITZY_PRODUCT_REFERENCE_ORDERING_V2_ENABLED`, whose default is false, so production has been sending the list's own row order. The planner reads the first eight products as the hero's references and hands the rest to the views, which only means something in priority order, so the loader sorts unconditionally and the flag is no longer read on the final render path (the config entry stays for now and is noted for removal with the other retired flags).
- Step 2, the camera read's `showsFocalElement: false` means "not in frame" (out of frame or behind the camera), not "behind the camera": the reviewer is told exactly that and asked to judge orientation against the room's geometry, and the code never escalates a focal fail unless the read says the element is in frame (review finding on the read commit; a third read value would over-ask a low-effort model for a distinction the verdict rules do not need).
